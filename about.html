I design and build embedded systems — the board, the firmware on it, and the enclosure around it. Most of what I make starts because something I wanted did not exist in the shape I wanted it, which is more or less how the Digital Card happened.

A paper business card is a thing you hand over and then both forget about. It seemed worth finding out whether the same object could carry a display, an antenna and a battery, still fit in a wallet, and still be cheap enough to give away. Eleven millimetres turned out to be the answer.

I graduated in Computer Engineering from Ajman University in December 2025, and spent a year before that leading the university's Computer Engineering Club — running workshops and competitions on Arduino, embedded systems and web development for students across the engineering disciplines.

The parts I enjoy most are the low-level ones: laying out a board, getting a state machine right in VHDL, working out why a clearance of 0.30 mm is fine and 0.20 mm is not. If you have something that needs designing rather than assembling from a kit, that is the work I want.
